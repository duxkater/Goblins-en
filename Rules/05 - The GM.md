Just as player-goblins trigger actions during the conversation, the GM has their own actions aimed at keeping the conversation fluid, exciting to play, and full of suspense.

Three tools assist the GM in this task:
- Agendas: these define the tone of the game and what the game is about.
- Principles: brief guidelines that frame the GM's decisions to ensure the agendas are upheld.
- GM Moves: these serve to drive the story forward while adhering to the principles.
## Objectives

There are three of them, and they should serve as a guide for all your decisions:
- Describe a warm, benevolent world from which goblins are excluded.
- Give the world a fantasy feel.
- Play to see how the clan influences the world.

**Describe a warm, benevolent world from which goblins are excluded.**
The cities the goblins will attack are teeming with life, resources, and people who rely on one another. You must make the players feel that the clan is shut out of these circles, and that the cities are ready to use force to preserve their way of life and wipe out the goblins. To humans, they are a nuisance.

**Give the world a fantasy feel.**
The game takes place in a world of magic, elves, dwarves, dark pacts with demons, and shamans sitting by the fireside—eyes rolled back—rasping out their visions.

**Play to see how the clan influences the world.**
The clan must survive in a world that is hostile to it. Its actions will impact the world, the sometimes delicate balance between factions, and the periods of plenty or scarcity experienced by the towns and villages.
## Principles

- Ask questions, use the answers.
- Act according to the fiction.
- Begin and end with the fiction.
- Be a fan of the characters.
- Immerse yourself in the fantastic.
- Address the characters, not the players.
- Don't name your moves.
- Think "danger."
## Moves

GM moves are triggered in three situations:
- When a player rolls a 6-
- When an opportunity arises
- When the players look to you to see what happens next

Here is the list of GM moves:
- Show signs of an approaching threat.
- Reveal an unwelcome truth.
- Bring the clan's curse into play.
- Point out the conditions or consequences of the PCs' actions.
- Offer the PCs an opportunity, with or without a cost.
- Put a fellow goblin in danger.
- Deplete the PCs' resources.
- Put someone in a difficult situation.
- Turn the PCs' actions against them.
- Separate the PCs.
- Deal damage.
## City Sheets

The GM's primary task is to keep the conversation flowing smoothly while adhering to the game's objectives. The content of the conversation is dictated by the **city sheets**.
A city sheet contains all the information the GM needs to run a game session.
It includes:
- **The city's name**
- A blank space to enter **the faction** to which it belongs.
- **Its size**, which determines the size of the scouting tracker and gives a rough idea of ​​the type of location the players wish to attack (a hamlet, a city, a capital, etc.).
- **Several impressions** to use occasionally to give the goblin players a sense of the city's atmosphere and add color to descriptions.
- **Clues**, presented as a list. Each clue can be revealed to the group when a specific action calls for it (usually the scouting action).
## Running the Scouting Phase

The scouting phase plays out like a standard role-playing game. Players describe their characters' actions, and you describe how the surrounding world reacts in turn.

However, keep in mind that this is an infiltration phase, which should be fraught with suspense and danger. The goblin players are on foreign turf, and they need to feel it. Townspeople flee from them, raise the alarm, or even grab the nearest makeshift weapon to drive them off.

Check off the clues the players discover as they go, without worrying too much about how they intend to use them. That is their problem to solve.
## Running the Raid Phase

During this phase, also played like a standard role-playing game, keep in mind that the town's level of chaos and alert rises in tandem with the raid tracker.

The start of this phase should catch the townspeople off guard; they are taken by surprise and likely overwhelmed by events. Then, as the tracker rises, the inhabitants rally, organize, and arm themselves, making things increasingly difficult for the goblins.

This is the phase where players have the opportunity to unlock each town's specific benefits, but only if they use the associated clue and the narrative situation allows for it.
