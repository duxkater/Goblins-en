A game session follows a fixed structure consisting of 5 phases, played sequentially.

These phases are as follows:

- Rest Phase
- Scouting Phase
- Planning Phase
- Raid Phase
- Victory Phase

Some phases are shorter than others, but all are important.
## Rest Phase

*(short phase)*

The Rest Phase is a time for goblins to boast, squabble, and proudly show off their latest treasure.
During this phase, follow these steps in order:

- The group chooses the next city to target.
- Each goblin player may resolve one condition.
- The GM sets up the scouting counter. Its value equals the target city's size minus the alert level of the faction controlling the city (minimum of 1).
    *E.g., for a size-4 city with a faction alert level of 2, the scouting counter will be 4 - 2 = 2.*
- The clan may spend 1 Wealth to increase the scouting counter by 1 point *(The goblins might have bribed guards, greased a lookout's palm...)*; they may do this as often as they wish, up to the limit of the clan's Wealth points.
- The clan spends 1 Food for each goblin player participating in the upcoming assault. A goblin who cannot eat suffers a persistent -1 penalty to all rolls until the next Victory Phase.
## Scouting Phase

*(long phase)*

During this phase, the goblin players are sent alone into the town they have chosen to attack. This is a "classic" role-playing phase where the GM and players converse, guided by the rules.

While infiltrating, the goblin players must find clues to help facilitate their upcoming raid, all without getting spotted.

**Failed moves and errors within the fiction increase the detection counter by 1 each time.**

When the counter reaches its limit, the goblins must retreat, often in a hurry. Their mischief has drawn too much attention from the authorities, and they are now being hunted.

Certain actions can lower the counter *(e.g., creating a diversion, taking out a lookout)*. If a player believes their action might lower the counter, they mention it to the GM, who decides whether to accept it.
## Planning Phase

*(short phase)*

The planning phase is when the goblin players must answer the following question:

**How ​​are we going to attack this place?**

This is the make-or-break moment: are the goblin players clever enough to capitalize on their discoveries, or will they rush headlong into disaster?

To answer this, they have access to all the clues gathered during the scouting phase. They are not required to use every clue, but the more they use, the easier the task will be in the next phase.

They can also seek advice from a clan elder, provided they have a connection to one. The elder might provide an additional clue that wasn't previously discovered, or invent a clue whispered to them by the spirits. (at the GM’s discretion).

Players also have the option to spend 1 point of their clan’s loot to obtain an additional clue. They have likely bribed a guard willing to look the other way.

The GM then creates a raid counter, the value of which equals the number of clues used.

Once the plan is set and the used clues are marked off, it is time to launch the attack.
## Raid Phase

*(long phase)*

The goblin players have alerted the clan and briefed everyone on the plan. It is time to swoop down on the town and plunder as much wealth as possible.

This is also a classic role-playing phase, where conversation, alongside the rules, drives the narrative.

During this phase, any action involving the use of an item is rolled with advantage. Each item can be used only once.

During this phase, any action involving a clue—in one way or another—is rolled with advantage. Each clue can be used only once.

**When a goblin player uses a clue, the raid tracker advances by 1.**

As the raid tracker fills up, the town reacts: panic spreads, defenders organize, reinforcements arrive...

When the raid tracker reaches its limit, the assault ends; the troops are exhausted, the town regroups, and it is time to make a run for it.

## Victory Phase

(short phase)

During this phase, the goblins savor their victory and count their spoils.

The GM adds the unlocked rewards to the clan sheet.

Each goblin transfers their loot tracker total to the clan's total.

The GM adjusts the faction trackers (increasing the tracker of the faction to which the raided town belongs by 1, and decreasing another tracker of their choice by 1 as well).

At this point, players may choose to consume loot units to add an equal amount of food or supplies.
